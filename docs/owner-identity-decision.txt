Single-owner identity and recovery decision

Status (2026-11-02): implemented with accepted private CI evidence; see
release-preparation.md and deploy/community/README.md for current recovery steps.
The design baseline below is historical. Use the community guide for commands;
references to proposed behavior describe the decision, not unfinished current work.
Source baseline: aea362783794e87ac3ea4a50a236f26e17233901.
Deployment boundary: see self-hosting-decision.txt.
Scope: one individual running a private library behind authenticated HTTPS.

Identity and access

Use one durable owner namespace, reusing the existing anonymous_sessions row and
its curation, upload and job associations. Pin its ID in protected persistent
operator configuration (implemented as OWNER_SESSION_ID / OWNER_SESSION_ID_FILE). It is a storage key,
not a password or bearer credential. Do not derive it from a cookie, signing key,
Basic Auth username, IP address, browser storage or request header. No account,
registration, invitation, team or role system is needed.

All private routes resolve this configured namespace. Caddy Basic Auth over HTTPS
authenticates the operator; the app port remains unpublished. Do not infer owner
authentication from a signed anonymous cookie or a caller-supplied Host/identity
header. The optional share ingress exposes only its narrow route allowlist and
never invokes owner resolution. This design depends on that ingress boundary;
the bare application is not an authenticated public service.

New and existing libraries

Provisioning explicitly initializes a random owner namespace once and persists
its ID. App/worker startup refuses missing, malformed or nonexistent owner
configuration instead of silently creating a replacement library identity.

For an existing library, require an operator-only, offline selection before the
first owner-mode start: inspect a read-only inventory of IDs, dates and curation/upload/job counts, then adopt
one identified legacy session or deliberately initialize an empty namespace.
Adoption pins the existing ID; it does not rewrite or copy its rows. A valid old
cookie may help identify a candidate offline, but neither a cookie nor the first
web visitor can perform adoption. Take a consistent backup before transition.

The selected session's favorites, archive flags, manual tags, notes, private
uploads and jobs become accessible from every authenticated browser. Preserve
media/message/job IDs, source links, files and visibility. Existing saved library
items remain library items. Other sessions, orphaned jobs and global/CLI curation
stay intact and separate; they are not automatically combined or exposed as owner
data. Initial support adopts one namespace, without multi-session consolidation.
Other namespaces require separately reviewed offline recovery. There is no web
claim endpoint, browser account switcher, wildcard adoption or last-write-wins
merge. This conservative limit avoids inventing ownership and resolving conflicting
notes or favorites without operator intent.

Durability and private drafts

Two authenticated browsers see the same owner curation, uploaded drafts and jobs.
Missing, invalid, expired or replaced cookies do not select a different owner.
All private item, original, thumbnail, keyframe, playback, job-image, recipe,
save and source-detail paths must use the same owner rule. Unselected legacy
private sources remain inaccessible, including nested list/detail/random metadata,
recipe content and job references. Preserve existing global server tags.

Provide authenticated discovery of uploaded drafts even when no job references
them, and retained job discovery beyond the current recent-job window. Drafts
remain private and outside the saved-library gallery until explicitly saved.
Saving adds an item to the private library; it does not create a public share,
even though existing database fields call saved items public/published.

Browser sessionStorage is only a workspace convenience. Unsubmitted selections,
analysis and tweak edits need not synchronize; submitted job settings and stored
drafts must recover from the server after browser storage is lost. A locally
dismissed job is not deleted. Worker execution preserves the configured owner
association across restart and only claims that namespace's eligible jobs and limits stale recovery mutations
to that namespace;
unselected/null legacy jobs are retained without automatic execution. Active-job
limits apply across the owner's browsers.

Recovery and sharing

Back up PostgreSQL, the complete reserve including drafts, the owner namespace
configuration, and separately protected signing/authentication material using the
consistent backup procedure. Restore into an empty instance and validate these
components before serving. Database and reserve recovery, not cookie recovery,
restores owner data. Loss of owner configuration requires explicit offline
identification of the retained namespace, never automatic selection.

Keep a persistent, strong PUBLIC_SESSION_SECRET. Restoring it preserves compatible
signed cookies and signed share URLs; it does not replace ingress credentials.
For loss or rotation, install a fresh strong secret consistently and restart the
relevant processes. Owner data and job ownership remain unchanged. Old signed
cookies and signed share URLs fail validation; no fallback-key scheme is needed.
Database-backed short share codes survive key rotation while their shares remain
active. Rotation is therefore not share recall: explicitly recall unwanted links.
Changing/resetting Basic Auth credentials also leaves ownership unchanged. A
restore of an older backup can restore old active-share state; keep the share
host disabled until that state has been reviewed.

Public sharing stays disabled by default and requires explicit enablement plus
an item share action. Public visitors receive only active authorized share
content, never owner curation or drafts. Sharing a remix output does not authorize
its private source originals, descriptions or recipe snapshots. Explicit recipe
publication must authorize the supported source presentation. Every public route,
alias and query variant must enforce that grant without exposing unapproved
drafts, private originals, unselected namespaces or owner curation, including
through descriptions and snapshots. Status lookup and private preview must not
expand publication scope. The optional-sharing implementation defines recipe
content eligibility and the effects of source recall, deletion and privatization;
this owner decision does not require separate source shares or whole-card denial.
Keep sharing gated until that policy is implemented and verified.

Required acceptance checks

- Two authenticated browsers, cookie deletion, invalid cookies and restart retain
  identical curation and access to standalone uploads, jobs and their private media.
- Adoption of session A preserves its IDs/data; session B and null-owned jobs
  remain unchanged and inaccessible. Repeated setup is safe; ambiguous selection,
  missing owner configuration and attempted web claiming cannot silently proceed.
  Include foreign stale jobs/attempts and nested source metadata in isolation checks.
- Unauthenticated private requests fail at ingress; public share visitors cannot
  reach owner APIs, raw media or drafts with any cookie, spoofed header or method.
- Output-only shares and private preview do not grant recipe/source access;
  explicit publication, recall, deletion and privatization follow the verified
  sharing policy. Save alone does not publish or create a share.
- Preserved/replaced signing keys, preserved/lost browser storage and restored
  short-code records produce the recovery behavior above without changing IDs.
- Isolated PostgreSQL concurrency/restart checks preserve job ownership and limits
  without blocking gallery reads; restored DB/reserve/config recover the library.

Use disposable fixture data and deterministic provider/queue stubs. No live data,
provider calls or Telegram activity belongs in these automated checks. This design
review traced committed source only; it does not claim these new checks have run.
